## First, the ground

Armenia sits high in the South Caucasus, a country of a few million people
and a very long memory. Its capital, Yerevan, is a city built from pink
volcanic stone, laid out on a grid that feels calm and readable after a week
or two of walking it.

For the nomad, the appeal is blunt: **a long, quiet visa, low costs, and a
calendar uninterrupted by crowds.** You are not competing for a table with
seasonal tourism. You are simply living somewhere pleasant while it goes
about its day.

## Staying, not visiting

The practical truth is that Armenia rewards patience more than planning. The
best café is discovered, not listed. The best view is the one you find while
losing your way home. After a month the city stops showing off and starts
being a home — which is precisely the point.

## What three months taught me

- Data and prepays are easy to source and cheap; the wifi hums along happily
  in the centre.
- Groceries are small-shop affairs; to know the bazaar is to know the week.
- Everyone has an opinion on the mountain view from their window, and they
  are all correct.
