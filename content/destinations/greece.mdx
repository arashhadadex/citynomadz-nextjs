## The pace wears off slowly

Greece's reputation is for party islands and summer heat. The reality we
keep returning to is quieter: **a coastline that is never far, a café culture
that respects its own time, and a season you can actually inhabit.**

Athens is the obvious long-stay base — walkable, cosmopolitan, and 
surprisingly drowsy outside the tourist corridors. But the real gift of
Greece is the pause it affords in the middle of the day. The siesta is not a
stereotype; it is a schedule, and one that suits a working wanderer well.

## Living on Mediterranean time

You learn to shop small, to eat with the season, and to let the afternoon go
soft. Work happens in the cool of the morning and the calm of the evening;
the bright middle hours belong to the sea and the shade.

## What three months taught me

- Internet is dependable in cities; move slightly out and plan for patience.
- The seasons matter more here than anywhere — July is a different country.
- Farmer's markets are the economy's honest heartbeat; go early.

The cost figures, dated and itemised, land in the journal with the full set
of field notes from the long stay.
