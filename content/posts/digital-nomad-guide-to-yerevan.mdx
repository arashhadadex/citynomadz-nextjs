Yerevan, the capital of Armenia, has quietly become one of the most
attractive destinations for digital nomads in Eastern Europe and the
Caucasus. With its blend of ancient history, a thriving cafe culture,
remarkably fast internet, and a cost of living that lets you live well on a
modest budget, it's easy to see why more remote workers are choosing Armenia
as their base.

This guide covers everything you need to know before packing your bags and
heading to Yerevan — from coworking spaces and accommodation to visas, food,
and local sim cards.

## Why Yerevan?

Armenia sits at the crossroads of Europe and Asia, and Yerevan reflects that
unique position. The city is walkable, safe, and filled with lively sidewalk
cafes, parks, and museums. But what makes it especially appealing for digital
nomads are the practicalities:

- **$800** — monthly cost of living
- **100+** — coworking seats across the city
- **180 days** — visa-free stay per year

## Visa & Entry

Citizens of over 100 countries — including the US, UK, Canada, EU, Australia,
and many others — can enter Armenia **visa-free for up to 180 days per
year**. That's one of the most generous visa policies in the region.

If you want to stay longer, Armenia offers a **one-year temporary residence
permit** that can be renewed. The process requires proof of income, a rental
agreement, and health insurance. Many nomads simply do a visa run to a
neighbouring country and return for another 180-day stamp.

> **Pro tip** — Always keep a digital or printed copy of your passport and
> entry stamp. You'll need it when registering with the immigration office for
> long-term stays.

## Internet & Coworking

Armenia has some of the **fastest and most reliable internet in the
Caucasus**. Most apartments offer fibre optic connections with speeds of
100 Mbps or more. Mobile data is cheap — you can get a 10 GB plan from
providers like Ucom or Viva-MTS for around **$5–7/month**.

### Best coworking spaces

- **Impact Hub Yerevan** — The most popular spot for nomads. Central
  location, great community, fast wifi, and regular events. Day pass: ~$10.
- **Loft** — A creative coworking space in a former factory building. High
  ceilings, plenty of natural light, and a relaxed vibe. Monthly membership:
  ~$120.
- **Work & Co** — A quieter option with several locations across the city.
  Good for focused work. Day pass: ~$8.
- **The Nine** — Part cafe, part coworking. Excellent coffee and reliable
  internet. Free with a purchase.

### Cafes with good wifi

If you prefer working from cafes, Yerevan won't disappoint. **Jazzve**
(multiple locations), **Coffeeshop Company**, and **Green Bean** all have
reliable wifi and a nomad-friendly atmosphere. The city center is packed with
outdoor terraces perfect for working during the warmer months
(April–October).

## Accommodation

Renting an apartment in Yerevan is one of the biggest draws for digital
nomads. You can find a **modern one-bedroom apartment in the city center for
$400–600/month**. Use Airbnb and Booking.com to search, or consider local
rental platforms like **list.am** for longer-term stays (1–3 months).

Popular neighbourhoods for nomads include:

- **Kentron (Center)** — The heart of the city. Walking distance to
  everything: Republic Square, cafes, coworking spaces, and shops.
- **Cascade Area** — Upscale neighbourhood around the Cascade complex.
  Beautiful views of Mount Ararat, quieter streets, and higher-end
  apartments.
- **Arabkir** — A bit further from the center but more affordable. Good bus
  connections and plenty of local shops.

## Food & Dining

Armenian food is a highlight of any visit. The cuisine is rich in fresh
vegetables, herbs, grilled meats, and — of course — lavash, the traditional
flatbread. Dining out is remarkably affordable:

- **Street food** (lahmacun, khorovats, fresh pastries): $2–5
- **Casual restaurant meal**: $8–15
- **Nice dinner with wine**: $20–35
- **Local beer in a bar**: $2–3
- **Armenian coffee**: $1–2

Don't miss trying **khorovats** (Armenian barbecue), **dolma** (stuffed grape
leaves), and **lavash** fresh from a tonir oven. For a truly local
experience, visit the **GUM Market** near Republic Square for fresh produce,
dried fruits, nuts, and sujukh (walnut fruit roll).

## Transport

Yerevan is a walkable city, but you'll likely use public transport at some
point. The **metro** (one line, ten stations) costs about $0.15 per ride.
Buses and minibuses (marshrutkas) cover the rest of the city for similarly
low prices. Ride-hailing apps like **Yandex Go** and **GG** are widely used —
a trip across the city typically costs $3–6.

For longer trips, Armenia's small size works in your favour. A taxi from
Yerevan to **Lake Sevan** is about $20 and takes 45 minutes. To **Gyumri**,
Armenia's second-largest city, it's about $30 and 90 minutes.

## Community & Events

Yerevan has a growing digital nomad community. Join the **Digital Nomads
Armenia** Facebook group to connect with other remote workers, find apartment
recommendations, and hear about meetups. There are regular events at Impact
Hub, language exchange meetups, and hiking groups that organize weekend
trips.

Speaking of hiking — Armenia is a paradise for outdoor lovers. The **Tatev
Monastery**, **Dilijan National Park**, and the volcanic landscapes of
**Mount Aragats** are all within a few hours' drive from Yerevan.

## Final Thoughts

Yerevan offers an impressive package for digital nomads: fast internet, low
costs, welcoming locals, and a rich culture to explore on weekends. The visa
policy makes it easy to stay long-term, and the city's size means you'll
never feel overwhelmed. Whether you're staying for a month or a year,
Armenia's capital has a way of making you feel at home.
